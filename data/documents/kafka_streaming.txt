Apache Kafka is a distributed event streaming platform used to publish, store, and consume streams of records.

Kafka organizes records into topics. Topics are divided into partitions, which provide parallelism and allow multiple consumers to process different partitions concurrently.

Producers publish records to Kafka topics. Consumers read records from topics and track their progress using offsets.

A consumer group allows multiple consumer instances to coordinate processing. Each partition is normally assigned to one consumer within a consumer group at a time.

Kafka retains records according to configured retention policies. Consumers can process records at their own pace within the available retention period.

Partitioning is important for Kafka scalability. Records with the same key are normally routed to the same partition, which can preserve ordering for that key.

Kafka is commonly used as an ingestion layer for streaming data pipelines. Downstream systems can consume events and transform, enrich, validate, or store them.
